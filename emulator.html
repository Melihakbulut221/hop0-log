A toy landing loop

Fly a small hop vehicle through wind, sensor noise, latency and lost frames, and see why the real loop is tested in hardware-in-the-loop. This is a teaching model with textbook control and generic round-number parameters. It is not the flight software and not Hop-0: none of its parameters, gains or laws are Hop-0's.

Fly one hop

The vehicle climbs, moves 40 m to the pad, hovers until it is over it, and descends. A landing counts if touchdown is below 2 m/s down and 1 m/s sideways, within 5 m of the pad and below 10° tilt. Change a setting, then press Fly.

Run a batch

One flight says little. These buttons fly 50 seeded flights with the settings above (seeds from the one set above), without drawing them, or repeat that for a range of latency or packet loss.

What to try: at the defaults the toy lands every time. Raise the latency step by step: the attitude loop keeps working until a point, then fails all at once. Then set the latency just below that point and sweep the packet loss: lost frames make the information older still, and the two compound. Wind matters only near the top of its range, sensor noise hardly at all. At the defaults, seeds 1 to 50: latency 150 ms still lands 49 of 50, 200 ms 12, 250 ms none; at 150 ms, 20% packet loss brings it to 37 and 40% to 6. These are toy numbers, computed in Chrome and node; another browser engine can differ by a run.

The real thing

Measured, not toy: the C flight software in hardware-in-the-loop on the Hop-0 plant, wind up to 12 m/s with gusts, from the current numbers. The toy cannot reproduce these; its job is to show why latency and lost frames get their own rows.

  • The real loop survives 200 ms and 15% packet loss because the flight software predicts across the latency and the lost frames; the toy's textbook controller does not, which is why it fails much earlier.
  • A fast simulation that did not model packet loss twice pointed the wrong way: a filter gain it recommended made the 20 Hz HIL worst case worse, 186 to 172 of 200 (which simulator to trust).

What the toy is

  • A planar rigid body (horizontal and vertical position, pitch) of 100 kg and 50 kg·m², constant mass, with one engine of up to 2000 N on a gimbal 1 m below the centre of mass, limited to ±8° and to the gimbal rate set above.
  • Quadratic drag (1.5 m² drag area) acting 0.5 m below the centre of mass, so a side wind tips the vehicle; wind is a mean plus a first-order gust process. In Hop-0 the large wind effect came from the CFD moment of the legs, which the toy does not model.
  • A textbook controller at 25 Hz: a PD position loop picks a tilt and a thrust, a PD attitude loop turns the tilt into a gimbal angle. No navigation filter, no prediction: the controller acts on noisy, late measurements, and when a frame is lost it keeps using the last one it received. Losses come in bursts of three frames on average.
  • Every number above is a round value chosen for this page. The real vehicle, flight software, gains, filter and guidance law are not published; Landing describes them at method level.
  • The page fixes the random streams by seed: a run is fully defined by its settings and its seed.
