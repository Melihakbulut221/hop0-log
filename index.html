Four results and where they come from

Each number below states the scenario, how many runs it rests on and where its table is.

159 → 300 of 300

Landings in the hardest software-in-the-loop scenario

After CFD aerodynamics replaced the simple drag model the lander fell from 293 to 159; one extra state in the navigation filter, a series of guidance changes and a correction of the filter's model of the vehicle brought it back. In percent: 53% to 100%; on the held-out seeds 99.7%.

0 of 800

Simulated leg drops inside the design envelope that tipped, bounced, bottomed or crushed a cartridge

400 drops at each of two propellant loads inside the envelope: up to 2 m/s down, 1 m/s sideways, 8° tilt, 5° slope. They are part of a 2640-drop grid that also covers harder drops outside the envelope, where the vehicle can tip. The negative finding: the legs need ground friction of 0.6 or below.

~1,400 flights

Before the first part wears out

The chamber's hot wall is the first part to wear out, after a median of 1,429 flights (780 flights if film cooling is at the pessimistic end). With the earlier software, one loss in 600 hardest-scenario flights made loss come first; the current software had no loss in 4400 SIL flights, which puts loss and wear in the same order.

What broke on the way

The twin exists to break things before they are built. These are four of the times it did, each with the entry that records it.

293 159

Real aerodynamics broke the lander

When CFD force tables replaced the simple drag model, landings in the hardest SIL scenario fell to 159 of 300. Getting back to 300 took a new filter state, guidance changes and a corrected vehicle model.

The filter forgot the legs

Heavier legs in the plant exposed a navigation filter that modelled the vehicle without its leg masses. With the fix the hardest scenario lands 300 and 299 of 300.

The method

How every number here is kept honest

The rocket is the example. The part that transfers to any hardware programme is the chain that has to hold before a result is allowed onto this site.

  1. One logic, three ways

    The flight logic exists as a Python reference and as C. The C matches the reference step by step to about 10-6 and gives the same outputs on an emulated Cortex-M7.

    Flight software

  2. Two simulators that must agree

    A fast in-process emulator reproduces the software-in-the-loop runs episode by episode. Changes are screened in the fast one and confirmed in the slow one.

    Which simulator to trust

  3. Held-out seeds

    A change counts only if it holds on a second, disjoint set of random seeds it was never tuned on.

    Seed sets A and B

  4. Independent review

    Board and analysis changes go through a review that can send them back. The inverter change was sent back twice before it was published.

    An example

  5. Numbers tied to sources

    Before anything is published, an automated check confirms that every number already published is still there or was removed with a stated reason, that headline sentences quote their source file, and that no restricted design data appears.

    The log

  6. Failures stay on the record

    Reverted changes, wrong claims and the "HIL" naming correction are dated entries in the log, next to the successes.

    A correction

How the twin is built

A twin usually mirrors a machine that already exists. This one comes first: the vehicle is designed, flown and broken in software, and only what survives gets built.

Six models run together. Each one is checked against something independent of it.

LayerModelChecked againstPage
PlantMuJoCo flight dynamics, with mass properties from CAD that change as propellant burns the CAD mass table it importsVehicle
AirRANS CFD force and moment tables from 0° to 180° angle of attack, legs included mesh-convergence and DDES checksVehicle
StructureFinite elements of tanks, legs, thrust structure and engine cooling, plus leg drop tests factors of safety 1.25 on yield, 1.4 on ultimateVehicle
GuidanceA classical controller, a multi-rate navigation filter and a predictive terminal law simulation and SIL on disjoint seed setsLanding
Flight codeC, MISRA checked the Python reference, to about 10-6; a Cortex-M7 in QEMUAvionics
LoopThe flight code as a separate process, over UDP, with latency, loss and sensor faults an in-process emulator that reproduces it episode by episodeAvionics

It is a twin in the engineering sense: the physics, the aerodynamics and the structure are modelled from the design, and the code it flies is the code the flight computer will run, not a stand-in for it.

Until there is hardware, it has nothing to sync with. Nothing on this site has flown. The twin also keeps a wear account for every simulated flight, and the same step will take real flights once there are any.

About

One person, the whole chain

Hop-0 is an independent project by Hasan Melih. It follows one small flight vehicle through every discipline it needs, and checks each against something independent of it.

The aim is to show how the work is done, end to end, before there is money for hardware. Questions, criticism and conversations about the method are welcome.
