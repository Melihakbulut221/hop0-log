  1. 2026-11-14Avionics

    PDU-1: review fixes for heavy copper, pad vias and the auxiliary-contact pulse

    Changed
    A stricter spacing rule for the heavy inner copper layers is now enforced by both the design-rule check and the project check script, and no via sits inside a component pad. The isolated switch drivers gained a return via so their short gate loops close through the plane beneath. The auxiliary-contact wetting circuit got a blocking diode, a faster reset and pulse-rated resistors, and a script now draws a coating and masking map from the layout. Still no rule violations or open connections.
    Review
    The review found bare stud pads close to opposite-polarity copper where the coating is masked, the power-supply input capacitors connected only through long repair tracks, and the repair step not exactly reproducible. Spacing, loop and temperature figures are estimates; not fabricated.

    PDU-1

  2. 2026-11-14Firmware

    Inverter firmware: gate-supply pre-charge, timer behaviour corrected

    Changed
    The half-bridge drivers need their high-side gate supply charged before the first high-side switching, so the firmware now runs a short sequence of low-side-only pulses across the three legs at every start, and a fault turns all outputs off at once.
    Review
    Review of the first version found that the microcontroller's timer drives a lone low-side output with the uninverted signal: the pulses would have been about five times longer than intended, and the change-over to normal switching could briefly use stale settings. The sequence was rebuilt around the documented timer behaviour and checked with a tick-level timer model across all timing cases. Each leg's first pulse still carries the start-up risk it exists to reduce. Bench measurement is still open.

    INV-1

  3. 2026-11-14Avionics

    PDU-1 layout closes: zero rule violations, zero open connections

    Changed
    The power-distribution board's layout is now electrically complete in the design tool. The stack-up changed to thinner outer and heavier inner copper, so the fine-pitch logic parts fit on the outer layers, and a widened logic strip replaced the idea of a separate logic board and its connector. Placement, gate-loop drawing and post-route repairs are scripted. A mis-coded isolated supply and an obsolete transistor were replaced, and the contactor's auxiliary contact now gets a short wetting pulse at each closure.
    Review
    The board rebuilds exactly from the saved autorouter session, but a fresh run may differ. The review found inner-layer spacing on the heavy copper below the usual fabrication minimum, vias inside a few small pads, and a discharge-path length misreported in the notes. Whether the wetting pulse meets the contactor maker's minimum is still to be asked. Not fabricated.

    PDU-1

  4. 2026-11-14Avionics

    Inverter: high-side gate supply margin recalculated

    Changed
    Review showed the earlier estimate of the high-side gate supply was too optimistic: it missed the drain during each on-time and the driver's own bias current. A per-period charge-balance model with datasheet worst cases now sets the design. Lighter gate pull-down resistors and a slightly lower duty-cycle limit keep the supply above the driver's undervoltage limit with a small calculated margin, at the cost of about 2 % top speed without field weakening.
    Review
    The numbers held, but the review found an older gap: the firmware never pre-charges that supply before the first switching cycle, which the driver's datasheet asks for. Not yet measured.

    INV-1

  5. 2026-11-14Avionics

    ECU-1: spacing rules, chassis ring, fabrication notes, and 23 connections open again

    Changed
    ECU-1's copper spacing now follows IPC-2221B by voltage: 0.6 mm on the outer layers between battery, armed-rail, valve-output and harness nets and everything else, with ten fine-pitch packages listed as mandatory conformal-coating areas. The design-rule check enforces both; run on the previous layout it flags about 500 spacing errors. A chassis copper ring with stitching vias runs along the edge, and draft fabrication notes fix the stack-up, including 1 oz inner copper, and a 2 µH valve-harness limit.
    Review
    Rerouting under the new rules left 23 connections open, and almost all of them break a function: the arm switch, the programming header, a chamber-pressure input and several sensors. The review also found two fault-exposed nets missing from the rules, and questioned whether 0.6 mm between low-voltage harness lines, which pushed most of those pins out of the router, is worth its cost. The board is a draft.

    ECU-1

  6. 2026-11-14Avionics

    Inverter board: reverse-trip margin, safer interlock, scripted layout

    Changed
    An independent check of the inverter's DC-link current found the normal full-power reverse current about a quarter higher than the project's own model, so the reverse-trip threshold was raised to about 1.5 times the worst normal current. The cost is that hardware protection against a phase-to-negative short now covers only short-to-medium wiring. The controller now caps the duty cycle so the high-side gate supply always recharges, and the driver-input interlock was rebuilt so one command blocks the other through settled signals, verified by simulating the real netlist.
    Review
    The review found the gate-supply margin overstated: with the driver's real bias current and the gate pull-downs it is marginal, not comfortable. A scripted run rebuilt the layout with every connection made, but a repeat run did not, so routing is not yet reproducible. Everything is calculated; nothing has been measured.

    INV-1

  7. 2026-11-14Avionics

    Inverter board: short-circuit detection moved inside the capacitor bank

    Changed
    The last review showed the inverter's DC-link shunt sits outside its own ceramic capacitor bank, so it sees a short late. Detection now uses the three low-side phase shunts, which carry the full fault current; each has a fast comparator that ignores the switching spike for a few hundred nanoseconds after turn-on. A reverse comparator on the DC-link shunt catches phase-to-negative shorts on short wiring, a logic interlock blocks a steady command to turn on both switches of a leg, and the dead time was lengthened. All connections are now routed.
    Review
    A time-domain model with the local bank shows shorts through 100 nH or more cut within the switches' rating; a solid stud short and long phase-to-negative shorts are not covered. The review found the reverse trip's margin against normal full-power current thin enough to trip by mistake, and the routing not yet reproduced from a clean run. Nothing has been tested on hardware.

    INV-1

  8. 2026-11-14Avionics

    Inverter board: new gate drivers, discrete over-current trip

    Changed
    The inverter's three-phase gate driver went out of production, and the planned replacement family turned out to be end-of-life as well. The board now uses one 180 V half-bridge driver per leg, placed between the paired switches, which cuts the driver-to-gate distance to about a third. The protection the old driver had built in is rebuilt from small parts: a comparator on the DC-link shunt sets a latch that blocks all gate inputs, and a separate supply lockout stops switching before the gate drive gets weak. Open connections fell from 62 to 7.
    Review
    The short-circuit survival table was wrong in method: the shunt sits outside the local capacitor bank, so a short is fed first by that bank and the trip sees it late. Survival is not shown, and that path will be reworked. The trip circuit's own supply and ground are among the 7 open connections, and the new driver is single-sourced. Not ready to fabricate.

    INV-1

  9. 2026-11-14Avionics

    ECU-1: valve power copper and a short-circuit inductance sweep

    Changed
    The valve supply never reached several pins because the board had no copper plane for it, only routed tracks too wide to land on fine-pitch pins. It now runs on dedicated internal copper regions and fixed copper in each valve cell. After automatic routing and a scripted, clearance-checked repair step, the design-rule check reports no violations and no open connections. A simulation that swept harness inductance from 0 to 2 µH showed the earlier over-current trip could exceed the switch rating on a very short wire; each output now has a 1 µH series inductor, a lower clamp and a slow shared retry clock.
    Review
    The figures rest on estimated component values, and a harness longer than about 2 to 3 m would break the thermal check. The review found a repaired chassis track running between the valve connector pins, 36 V spacing that needs conformal coating or wider rules, and an inner copper thickness that must be specified. The board is not fabricated.

    ECU-1

  10. 2026-11-14Avionics

    Inverter board: harness protection both ways, two routing errors found

    Changed
    The inverter's fault line now has a series resistor sized together with the engine controller's pull-up, so a sustained short on it is limited in either polarity. The 12 V supply got a hold-up bank sized for the estimated load and the worst-case restart of the overvoltage switch, and each harness line now has its own clearance rule. Checking the routed board found two autorouter errors that had cut one low-side switch off from its current shunt; both were fixed, and every gate pad is connected. Open connections fell from 95 to 62.
    Review
    An independent review found the ride-through model losing charge, so it overstated the lowest supply voltage. Corrected, the supply still stays above the gate driver's undervoltage limit, in the calculation only. The gate driver is obsolete; a per-leg replacement is being evaluated, and most of the open connections wait for it.

  11. 2026-11-14Avionics

    ECU-1: a hardware over-current trip on the valve outputs

    Changed
    Each valve and igniter output gained a fast comparator on its sense resistor. Above about 5 A it clears a per-channel latch that holds the driver off until the next PWM edge, and the driver now also needs the hardware arm signal, so firmware alone cannot energise a valve. The data-bus protection was replaced with parts rated for a sustained short to the 36 V battery, and the inverter fault-line pull-up was retuned for a matching inverter change.
    Review
    An independent review found the trip analysis used the most forgiving wiring inductance: a very short, low-inductance short could still overstress the switch. It also found 21 connections still open after rerouting, including three signal nets and the valve connectors' supply pins. The board is not ready for fabrication, and the trip timing is an estimate, not a measurement.

    ECU-1

  13. 2026-11-13Site

    Correction: the loop is software-in-the-loop, not hardware-in-the-loop

    Changed
    Every landing number this site called "HIL" came from the real C flight code running on a PC, talking to the simulator over UDP. With no flight computer in the loop, that is software-in-the-loop (SIL). The site now says SIL everywhere; none of the numbers changed.
    Still true
    The code in the loop is the flight code, not a stand-in, and it gives the same outputs on an emulated Cortex-M7. Hardware-in-the-loop starts when the FC-0 board exists; the loop already speaks the protocol the board will use.

  15. 2026-11-12Avionics

    Inverter gate networks moved next to the switches; the routing got harder, not easier

    Changed
    Every gate resistor, turn-off diode and pull-down on INV-1 now sits within about 6 mm of its switch, down from 20 to 50 mm; the inductance between the gate resistor and the gate fell about eightfold. The harness entry got the same short-to-battery protection as the engine controller, with a logic-level enable transistor that keeps more than 1 V of margin at -40 C.
    Did not improve
    The aim was fewer open connections. There are 95, up from 84: wider harness clearances and the protection parts took routing room. The total gate loop did not shrink either, because the driver's position sets it.
    Review
    An independent review sent the change back twice: once for a temperature grade that could not survive -40 C, a restart delay that would stop the pump on every supply pulse and harness lines with signal-level spacing, and once because the text claimed the pump rides through a pulse, which the arithmetic does not yet show.

  16. 2026-11-12Avionics

    The engine controller survives a shorted harness, and two old bugs fall out

    Changed
    Every input on ECU-1, and the enable, fault and supply lines to both inverters, now survive a sustained short to the vehicle battery or to a reversed battery. The board was re-placed and re-routed.
    Found
    Working through the short cases exposed two errors in the earlier schematic: the optical flame sensor was wired with reversed polarity, so the optical proof of ignition could never have read true, and both RTD channels had their excitation on the wrong pin and would have read open. Both are fixed. The review also found that one pressure wire shorted to the battery can set the hardware fuel permit on its own; the firmware now refuses to arm unless those comparators read idle before ignition.
    Still open
    The valve power outputs and the CAN lines are not protected yet. 25 connections remain to route by hand, two of them signals.

  18. 2026-11-10Navigation

    The filter modelled the vehicle without its leg masses

    Found
    The navigation filter, in Python and in C, read mass, inertia and centre of mass from the simulator's hull body. Since plant v4 the simulator carries the moving part of each leg as a separate body at the foot, so the filter's vehicle had too little pitch inertia and a centre of mass too high, and its gimbal torque model predicted more angular acceleration than the vehicle had. The heavier legs of plant v5 made it visible.
    Isolated
    Emulator, hardest scenario, 300 episodes: new legs with the filter reading the hull body 275; with the filter reading the whole vehicle 300; filter and controller scaling both on the whole vehicle 300; old legs with the whole-vehicle filter 300 (299 with the hull body).
    Changed
    The filter uses the composite mass properties of the whole vehicle. The controller's gain scaling stays on its tuning reference, since the composite variant gave the same result. C parameters and traces regenerated; the equivalence checks pass.
    Measured
    SIL, plant v5: hardest scenario 300 (set A) and 299 (set B) of 300; worst aerodynamic corner 293 and 299 of 300 (592 of 600, was 574 on plant v4); no radar 298 of 300; 20 Hz hardest scenario 199 and 199 of 200, 20 Hz with 150 + 50 ms latency 100 of 100. COTS and harsh rows 100 of 100.
    Lesson
    This supersedes the conclusion of the entry on the worst aerodynamic corner below: its failures were largely this model error, not latency. In that corner failures by mean wind are now 0 of 395 up to 8 m/s, 1 of 103 at 8-10, 2 of 59 at 10-11 and 5 of 43 at 11-12 m/s; the landing wind limit there is 10 m/s (99.8%) and the 8 m/s recommendation is withdrawn.

  20. 2026-11-10Structure

    Leg struts resized for their own inertia (CAD v0.4)

    Found
    With the struts' own sideways inertia and the corrected swinging mass, the first struts fall below their factors of safety: −0.19 for the primary, −0.42 for the secondaries, without fairings.
    Changed
    Fairing-free legs with Ø50 x 2 mm primary and Ø35 x 2 mm secondary struts; CAD v0.4 becomes the baseline: dry 91.4 kg (6.3% over the 86.0 kg budget), 109.7 kg with growth, four legs 15.5 kg in CAD against a 12.0 kg budget. The site had shown 89.8 kg dry (4.4% over), 107.7 kg with growth, from a fairing-free estimate with the first struts.
    Measured
    Lowest strut margin +0.18 (secondary beam-column, nonlinear FE); the pins at +0.09 are now the lowest margin of the leg. Drop campaign re-run, 1320 drops per propellant load: 0 tip-overs, bounces, bottoming or crushing in the 800 drops inside the envelope.
    Open
    A whole-tripod model gives the first two leg modes at 77 and 83 Hz only with every joint at 50 kN/mm (assumed); at 10 kN/mm the first is 54 Hz. The earlier single-strut estimate (about 65 Hz for the primary, first estimated at 140–150 Hz) missed the foot swinging on the joint springs; in the same tripod model the first struts reach 60 Hz. Zero-clearance joints with a measured stiffness of at least about 40 kN/mm each, and a modal test, are now requirements. Leg modes 3 and 4 (102 and 113 Hz) sit in the hull's bending band.

  22. 2026-11-10Boards

    A damper on the inverter, and the capacitor board follows

    Changed
    INV-1 gained an RC damper across its DC link, its DC studs moved to 12 mm apart, and its ceramic capacitors moved next to the switches. CB-1 was re-laid out to land on the new studs.
    Measured
    The capacitor-network resonance peak dropped about tenfold; the gate driver's high-side offset pin stays at or below 118 V across the swept wiring inductance. The ceramic capacitors now sit about 5 mm from the switch tabs instead of 31-37 mm. An independent model reproduced the damper within 0.1 V.
    Did not work
    The first damper was sized for a 5 mm post; the real stud is 7 mm, which left the resistors without margin. The re-selected damper keeps the margin but dissipates about 9 W. The gate resistors still sit 22-49 mm from the switches and 84 connections are open: not ready to build.

    The engine controller reads the power unit

    Changed
    ECU-1 gained a connector for PDU-1's status: three opto-coupled lines (pre-charge done, contactor closed, fault latch) and three analog monitors. The flight-computer arm vote input got a clamp and a transient suppressor.

  25. 2026-11-10Guidance

    Where the worst aerodynamic corner fails, and a tuning dead end (superseded)

    Measured
    In 600 SIL episodes with hull drag ×1.6 and leg drag ×2, failures rise with wind: none below 4 m/s, 1% at 4-8, 5% at 8-10, 15-23% above 10 m/s. Nineteen of the 26 failures are above 10 m/s.
    Did not work
    Eight variations of the lateral acceleration and terminal tilt limits, 800 emulator episodes each: none moved the result beyond noise. The remaining failures are latency and the aerodynamics itself.
    Decided
    Until the aerodynamic uncertainty is measured, the landing wind limit is 8 m/s (99% in this corner).
    Superseded
    The conclusion was wrong: most of these failures were the filter's vehicle-model error, found the same day. The wind limit in this corner is now 10 m/s; see the filter entry.

  28. 2026-11-10Life twin

    Life twin v0.1: a wear account per flight

    Changed
    Every flight record is turned into damage for each part that wears out. Built from 600 SIL episodes of the hardest scenario (both seed sets), flown by the software before the attitude-gain change above.
    Measured
    The chamber's hot wall wears out first, after about 1,400 flights (780 with pessimistic film cooling). Leg loads replayed at the physics rate matched the SIL log for 561 landings. One loss in 600 flights.
    Found

  29. 2026-11-10Orbit study

    How small an orbital launcher can be

    Changed

    Measured
    About 2.1 t is the smallest vehicle that reaches orbit, with 1 kg (2.9 t with 5 kg): a demonstrator. The recommended first product is about 23 t (range 20-31 t) for about 300 kg to 500 km. The simulator over-predicts a flown two-stage launcher by about 15%, carried as extra margin.

    At 20 Hz no candidate beat the noise and the best one went opposite ways on the two SIL seed sets. At 50 Hz the gain gave +3 of 600, at the noise limit, while reducing stability margin. Neither was applied. (The gain was applied on 2026-11-10 after a sweep on a different scenario.)
  38. 2026-11-07Guidance

    20 Hz with harsh sensors: tried, reverted twice

    Tried
    Filter and guidance settings for the 20 Hz reference configuration, first from the fast Python loop, then from the new emulator.
    Did not work
    The filter gain the Python loop recommended made the SIL hardest case worse, 186 to 172 of 200: the Python loop did not model packet loss. A second candidate gained +1 on a second seed set and cost 3 landings in a deterministic 50 Hz scenario. Both reverted. One seed block had been used in screening and in the "held-out" SIL set; since then the sets are disjoint.

    Terminal tilt limits for 12 m/s wind

    Found
    The remaining failures arrived at the terminal gate still drifting sideways; the tilt allowed near the ground was too small to stop that drift and hold against the wind.
    Changed
    Wider tilt limits near the ground.
    Measured

  44. 2026-11-04Aerodynamics

    Leg fairings: designed, checked, dropped

    Tried
    Free-swivelling leg fairings, designed and checked with finite elements after fixed fairings had been rejected: in crossflow they act as fins (1.9–3.4 times the lateral leg force in a strip model; CFD later gave 2.3–2.6 times).
    Measured
    CFD of the faired legs: only a 9–14% leg-force reduction in hover side wind, because about 60% of the leg force comes from parts that cannot be faired. In simulation with their mass (5.8 kg of parts, 6.8 kg at vehicle level with growth): harsh 364 to 353 of 400, COTS 381 to 363.
    Decision
    Dropped. The extra mass lowers the centre of mass and shortens the gimbal's lever arm by more than the aerodynamics give back.

  45. 2026-11-04Navigation

    One extra state in the navigation filter

    Found
    A "±10° gimbal helps" study result was an artifact: the filter kept its own ±7° constant and under-modelled gimbal torque by 30%, which happened to offset the aerodynamic moment it did not know about.
    Changed
    An angular-acceleration disturbance state, learnt from the gyro residual, in Python and C together; the ±10° gimbal applied as well.
    Measured
    SIL hardest scenario 159 to 266 of 300; no radar 192 to 281; COTS 75 to 96 of 100; harsh 72 to 99. On its own the bigger gimbal changes the rate within noise.
    Found
    A C bug: the new state was not reset between episodes. Fixed; C/Python equivalence back within tolerance.

  49. 2026-11-03Structure

    Legs, aft body and deck redesigned

    Changed
    Inverted-tripod legs, a thicker base frame with doubler plates, a ribbed avionics deck with struts to the pressure vessel.
    Measured
    0 tip-overs, bounces, bottomed shocks or crushed cartridges in the 800 drops inside the envelope (400 per propellant load; the full grid of 1320 per load also covers drops outside it). Landing-case margins +0.30 or better. Deck first mode 163 Hz.
    Found
    Friction: at a coefficient of 1 the peak deceleration reaches 17.5 g; a low-friction landing surface (0.6 or below) was recommended; the next day's leg study made it a condition of its margins. A stiff ring at the nose joint is still needed.

  50. 2026-11-03Structure

    First finite-element pass: four findings against the design

    Found
    The regenerative cooling exceeded its wall-temperature limits because of an error in the cooling model. The first legs could not carry foot friction. The aft body had negative margins at the leg attachments. The flat avionics deck resonated where the 50 Hz loop folds its noise.
    Measured
    Tanks lowest margin +0.25, buckling +6.8, hull first bending mode about 100 Hz.
    Then
    The cooling model was corrected and the chamber redesigned; it now depends on a film-cooling effectiveness that must be measured before such a chamber is built.

  51. 2026-11-03Boards

    Engine controller and pump inverters

    Changed
    ECU-1 with a hardware arming chain and a fuel-valve interlock to proof of ignition; INV-1 pump inverters with field-oriented control.
    Measured
    Schematics ERC clean. In simulation the controller goes to the right abort in all of its fault scenarios, and the inverter firmware drives a motor and pump model through a full hop.
    Found

    Changed
    The simulator's first-order throttle model is replaced by the rate-limited response of the transient engine and controller model, in the plant and in the navigation filter's thrust prediction.

  53. 2026-11-03Guidance

    Aerodynamic-moment estimator in the controller: rejected

    Tried
    Estimating the aerodynamic moment from the derivative of the filtered body rates and feeding it forward.
    Measured
    Paired test, 600 episodes: in the hardest centre-of-pressure case failures fell from 20 to 9; in the realistic spread they rose from 2 to 7.
    Decision
    Rejected: the derivative carried sensor noise into the attitude loop. The better route, a disturbance state inside the filter without a derivative, came the next day.

    Measured

  55. 2026-11-03Plant

    The simulator ignored the centre of mass

    Found
    A MuJoCo body-frame flag kept the centre of mass at the hull's geometric centre. The gimbal lever arm was too long and the aerodynamic moment was always zero. Every guidance and SIL result before this date was measured on the wrong plant.
    Measured
    On the corrected plant the previous controller landed 37% to 44.5% of 200 episodes.
    Changed
    Retuned attitude gains and integral authority. Python on unseen seeds: 397 to 400 of 400 across sensor classes at 50 and 20 Hz.
